# RULES.md — Borrower Copilot domain rules

Every number the engine produces traces back to a rule in this table. None of these values are RBI-mandated (RBI does not fix a single FOIR or interest-rate number) except where marked; the rest are modelling judgement calls made for this assignment, chosen to be defensible and conservative rather than universally "correct." All constants live in `engine/foir.go` and `engine/rates.go`.

| # | What | Value | Why | Source / Judgement |
|---|------|-------|-----|---------------------|
| 1 | Base FOIR — salaried | 45% | Midpoint of the commonly-cited 40–50% salaried FOIR range; salaried income is the most verifiable and stable, so it anchors the middle of the band. | Judgement, per spec's stated 40–50% salaried range |
| 2 | Base FOIR — self-employed (with ITR) | 40% | One notch below salaried: income is provable via ITR but less stable month to month. | Judgement |
| 3 | Base FOIR — informal / cash income | 30% | Matches the spec's stated floor for informal workers; no verifiable income trail, so obligations must consume a smaller share. | Judgement, per spec's stated ≤30% informal floor |
| 4 | FOIR floor (absolute) | 20% | Even a heavily-penalized profile is given some notional EMI room rather than being clamped to a nonsensical negative FOIR; the actual gate against overborrowing is the verdict logic (rule 32–34), not this floor. | Judgement |
| 5 | FOIR ceiling (absolute) | 60% | Matches the spec's stated ceiling for high-income earners. | Spec-stated ceiling |
| 6 | High-income threshold | ₹2,00,000/month net | Round, defensible "clearly affluent" cutoff for metros; above this, fixed obligations are more sustainable because discretionary spend is a smaller share of income. | Judgement |
| 7 | High-income FOIR bonus | +15 points | Sized so a salaried high earner (45 base) can reach the spec's 60% ceiling. Not applied to informal earners — the spec ties the 60% relaxation to "high-income earners," which in practice means verifiable high income. | Judgement |
| 8 | Good-credit threshold | CIBIL ≥ 750 | Standard industry cutoff for "prime" borrowers used across Indian lenders. | Industry convention |
| 9 | Good-credit FOIR bonus | +5 points | Demonstrated repayment discipline earns modest extra room. | Judgement |
| 10 | Bad-credit threshold | CIBIL < 650 | Standard industry cutoff below which most lenders consider a borrower sub-prime. | Industry convention |
| 11 | Bad-credit FOIR penalty | −10 points | Matches the spec's note that bad credit should drop FOIR toward 30% or lower. | Judgement, per spec |
| 12 | Bounce penalty | −10 points per bounced payment in the last 3 months | A recent bounce is the single strongest predictor of near-term distress; penalized per-occurrence so multiple bounces compound. | Judgement |
| 13 | Emergency-savings threshold | ≥ 3 months of expenses saved | Standard personal-finance rule of thumb for a minimum buffer. | Industry convention |
| 14 | Emergency-savings FOIR bonus | +5 points | A cushion means a temporary income shock doesn't immediately cause a missed EMI. | Judgement |
| 15 | Variable-income discount | Variable/cash share of income counted at 50% value | Cash flow that isn't guaranteed shouldn't be treated as equivalent to fixed salary when sizing a fixed monthly obligation; halving is a simple, conservative haircut rather than a fitted statistical discount. | Judgement |
| 16 | Residual-income safety factor | 80% of (income − household expenses − existing EMIs) | Leaves a 20% buffer over the bare survival number so an EMI doesn't consume every last rupee of headroom; this is the check that actually protects low-margin, informal-income borrowers, since their FOIR-based number alone can look deceptively affordable. | Judgement |
| 17 | Bank FOIR (sanction-side) — salaried / self-employed / informal | 50% / 45% / 35% | Banks' own underwriting formulas are typically looser than a "safe" number because they check income against FOIR only, not against a borrower's actual household budget. Used only for the Sanction Limit, never the Safe Capacity Limit. | Judgement |
| 18 | Collateral loan-to-value (LTV) | 50% | Typical conservative LTV for loan-against-property/gold in Indian secured lending; caps the Sanction Limit whenever collateral is offered. | Industry convention |
| 19 | Processing fee | 2% of principal | Matches the spec's "standard 2% processing fee" instruction for the all-in APR calculation. | Spec-stated |
| 20 | Unsecured rate band — salaried | 11%–13% | Representative of prime salaried personal-loan pricing from Indian banks/NBFCs before individual adjustments. | Market-representative judgement |
| 21 | Unsecured rate band — self-employed | 14%–18% | Loaded above salaried for harder-to-verify income. | Market-representative judgement |
| 22 | Unsecured rate band — informal | 24%–36% | Deliberately placed in the same territory as high-cost digital/app lending, because that is where an informal borrower without documentation actually prices in today's market — the engine doesn't pretend a cheaper rate is available. | Market-representative judgement |
| 23 | Secured rate band (LAP / gold / vehicle-lien) | 9%–11% | Collateral is the dominant risk mitigant for these products, so pricing sits near secured-loan territory regardless of employment type. | Market-representative judgement |
| 24 | Unknown credit score adjustment | Band shifts −2/+8 and is flagged "wide" | Per the assignment's core constraint: an unknown must never default to an average — it is priced as elevated risk and the band is widened, not centered. | Spec-mandated design principle |
| 25 | Good credit score adjustment | Band shifts −1.5/−2 | Tightens and lowers the band for demonstrated good credit. | Judgement |
| 26 | Bad credit score adjustment | Band shifts +3/+6, flagged "wide" | Sub-650 credit both raises and widens the band — real repayment behaviour is uncertain even within "bad." | Judgement |
| 27 | Bounce rate adjustment | +2 points on the top of the band per bounce | Recent missed payments raise the ceiling a lender would realistically quote. | Judgement |
| 28 | APR formula | `APR = ((Total Interest + Processing Fees) / Principal) × (365 / Tenure in Days) × 100` | Exact formula specified by the assignment; folds the mandatory processing fee into the annualised cost of credit. Quoted at the conservative (upper) edge of the fair-rate band so the borrower plans for the worse case, not the best case. | Spec-stated formula |
| 29 | EMI formula | Standard reducing-balance annuity: `EMI = P·r·(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1)` | Standard formula used by every Indian lender for term loans. | Industry-standard formula |
| 30 | Safe Capacity Limit | `MaxPrincipal(EMI ceiling, high edge of rate band, tenure)` | The loan size the borrower's own EMI ceiling can support, priced conservatively. | Derived |
| 31 | Sanction Limit | `MaxPrincipal(bank FOIR × income, mid rate band, tenure)`, capped at collateral LTV if collateral is offered | Approximates what a bank's own (looser) formula would approve — always shown alongside, never in place of, the Safe Capacity Limit. | Derived |
| 32 | Verdict gate 1 — underwater | Income − household expenses − existing EMIs ≤ 0 ⇒ **Don't borrow** | If current obligations already exceed income, no new EMI can be serviced regardless of loan size. | Judgement, safety-first |
| 33 | Verdict gate 2 — bounce + thin margin | ≥1 bounce **and** EMI ceiling < 5% of income ⇒ **Don't borrow** | A recent bounce plus almost no safe room is the assignment's explicit "actively tell high-risk profiles not to borrow" case. | Spec-mandated, judgement on threshold |
| 34 | Verdict gate 3 — no capacity | EMI ceiling ≤ 0 or Safe Capacity ≤ 0 ⇒ **Don't borrow** | Catches any remaining zero/negative-capacity case not caught by gates 1–2. | Judgement |
| 35 | Verdict — fits | Safe Capacity ≥ amount wanted ⇒ **Borrow** | Requested amount fits within what the borrower can safely repay. | Derived |
| 36 | Verdict — partial fit | Safe Capacity < amount wanted (and no gate triggered) ⇒ **Borrow less** | Recommends scaling the request down to the Safe Capacity Limit rather than a flat refusal. | Derived |
| 37 | Secured-product routing | Non-salaried **and** collateral offered **and** an unsecured product was requested ⇒ recommend the secured product instead, and price/size using the secured band | Implements the spec's requirement to route asset-rich, credit-invisible borrowers (e.g. Ravi) to LAP/secured lending rather than quoting them an unaffordable unsecured rate. | Spec-mandated |
| 38 | Confidence flag | **Low** iff zero additional (tier-2) questions were answered; **High** if even one was answered | Matches the spec literally: "if only these [core] questions are answered, output... a low confidence flag." Answering even a single high-impact question (e.g. disclosing a bounce) means the engine is no longer guessing on that dimension. | Spec-stated |
| 39 | Stress tests | Recompute EMI ceiling at (a) income × 0.8, (b) rate + 2 points; compare against the EMI this specific loan would require | Directly implements the spec's required stress-test scenario. | Spec-stated |
| 40 | Co-applicant pooling | Co-applicant's income added to pooled income if disclosed | Widens capacity but the UI/negotiation card notes joint liability — pooling income without pooling risk would be misleading. | Judgement |
| 41 | Collateral question relevance | Asked for any non-salaried borrower, or any borrower (salaried included) requesting ≥ ₹5,00,000 | Keeps the adaptive questionnaire from asking irrelevant questions (e.g. a salaried borrower wanting a small personal loan almost never needs to discuss collateral) while still surfacing it once the stakes are large enough to matter. | Judgement |
| 42 | Variable-income-share question relevance | Only asked of non-salaried borrowers | A salaried income is definitionally fixed; asking about "variable share" for a salaried borrower can never move a number, so it is skipped per the spec's "don't ask a question that doesn't move a number" rule. | Spec-mandated design principle |

| 43 | Path to Yes (counterfactual levers) | For a "Borrow less"/"Don't borrow" verdict, independently re-run the same core calculation (rules 1–36) with one input changed at a time — existing EMIs cut, CIBIL raised to 750, savings built to 3 months, or the product switched to secured if collateral is disclosed — and report what safe capacity and the verdict become | Answers "what would need to change to safely get the loan?" using only the existing model, never a separate score; each lever is evaluated in isolation (others held at their real values) so the reported effect is honest about what one change alone does, including when it isn't enough. | Derived from rules 1–36; no new constants |

## Explicit non-goals / things this table does not claim
- These bands and FOIR numbers are **not** a substitute for an actual lender's credit policy; they are a self-assessment heuristic to prepare a borrower to negotiate, not an approval guarantee.
- Tenure (months) is treated as a 9th core (must-answer) question even though the original brief listed 8 — it is mathematically required by both the EMI and APR formulas and cannot be defaulted without violating rule 24's "never default an unknown" principle.
